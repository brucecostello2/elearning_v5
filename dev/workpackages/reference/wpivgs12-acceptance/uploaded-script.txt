# How to Multiply Double-Digit Numbers

Hi! Today, we’re going to learn how to multiply **two-digit numbers**.

That might sound tricky, but don’t worry. We’re going to break it into small, easy steps.

By the end, you’ll be able to solve a problem like **23 times 14** all by yourself.

Ready? Let’s begin!

## Step 1: Set Up the Problem

Our problem is:

**23 times 14.**

Write 23 on top, and 14 underneath it. Make sure the ones digits line up, and the tens digits line up.

So, the 3 is above the 4, and the 2 is above the 1.

Draw a line underneath.

Now we’re ready to multiply.

## Step 2: Multiply by the Ones Digit

We start with the **4** in 14.

First, multiply 4 times 3.

**4 times 3 equals 12.**

Write the 2 underneath the ones column, and carry the 1 above the tens column.

Next, multiply 4 times 2.

**4 times 2 equals 8.**

But remember, we carried a 1.

So we add it:

**8 plus 1 equals 9.**

Write down the 9.

Our first answer is **92**.

Great! That means 23 times 4 equals 92.

## Step 3: Multiply by the Tens Digit

Now we need to multiply by the **1** in 14.

But there’s something very important to remember.

That 1 isn’t really just 1.

Because it’s in the tens place, it means **10**.

So before we start, put a **zero** in the ones place on our new answer line.

This zero is a placeholder. It reminds us that we’re multiplying by tens.

Now multiply 1 times 3.

**1 times 3 equals 3.**

Write down 3.

Then multiply 1 times 2.

**1 times 2 equals 2.**

Write down 2.

Our second answer is **230**.

That makes sense, because 23 times 10 equals 230.

## Step 4: Add the Two Answers

We’re almost finished!

We have two answers:

**92**

and

**230.**

Now add them together.

2 plus 0 equals 2.

9 plus 3 equals 12.

Write down 2 and carry the 1.

Then 1 plus 2 equals 3.

Our final answer is:

**322.**

So:

**23 times 14 equals 322.**

Nice work!

## Let’s Try Another One

Let’s practice with:

**32 times 21.**

Write 32 on top and 21 underneath.

Start with the ones digit, which is 1.

1 times 2 equals 2.

1 times 3 equals 3.

So our first answer is **32**.

Now move to the tens digit, which is 2.

Remember, because we’re multiplying by the tens digit, start by putting down a zero.

2 times 2 equals 4.

2 times 3 equals 6.

That gives us **640**.

Now add:

**32 plus 640 equals 672.**

So:

**32 times 21 equals 672.**

## The Four-Step Trick

Whenever you multiply two-digit numbers, remember these four steps:

**One:** Multiply by the ones digit.

**Two:** Start the next line with a zero.

**Three:** Multiply by the tens digit.

**Four:** Add the two answers together.

That’s it!

If you make a mistake, don’t worry. Check each multiplication one at a time, make sure your numbers are lined up correctly, and remember that important zero when you start multiplying by the tens digit.

With a little practice, multiplying two-digit numbers gets much easier.

Great job! You’ve just learned how to multiply two-digit numbers.
